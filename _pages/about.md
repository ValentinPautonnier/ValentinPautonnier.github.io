I am a SSHRC Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Political Studies at the [University of Ottawa](https://www.uottawa.ca/), where I work under the supervision of Professor Daniel Stockemer. I hold a PhD in Political Science from the Université de Montréal (2026).

My research examines how geography shapes electoral politics. I study the growing territorial divergence of Western electorates, the rural-urban cleavage, and the ways in which regional economic disparities, place identity, and place-based resentment influence vote choice, polarization, and public opinion. My work combines large-scale electoral and survey data with quantitative methods, in a comparative perspective spanning Canada, the United States, and Europe.

My doctoral dissertation, *The Geographic Reconfiguration of Politics: Drivers, Mechanisms, and the Territorial Divergence of Western Electorates*, analyzed the factors behind the spatial polarization of voting patterns across Western democracies.

Research interests
======
- Electoral behaviour and electoral geography
- Regional economic disparities and territorial inequalities
- Political polarization and public opinion
- Comparative politics
- Survey methodology

Recent work
======
My research has appeared in *Electoral Studies* and *Party Politics*, and is forthcoming in *The Journal of Politics*. Current projects examine place identity and place resentment in Canadian elections, the regional drivers of climate backlash, the spread of misinformation by electoral candidates, and the effect of democratic interventions on turnout. See my [publications](/publications/) page for details.

Teaching
======
I have taught undergraduate courses in Public Opinion and Political Marketing at the Université de Montréal. See my [teaching](/teaching/) page.

Background
======
Before joining the University of Ottawa, I was a Fellow of the Consortium on Analytics for Data-Driven Decision-Making (CanD3) at McGill University and a research intern at the Institute for Research on Public Policy (IRPP). I hold master's degrees in Political Sociology from Université Paris 1 Panthéon-Sorbonne and in Opinion Polling and Media Marketing from Sciences Po Grenoble.
